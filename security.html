Browser Security and Privacy

Browsers handle personal information and connect users to websites around the world. Security features help protect devices and information, while privacy features give users more control over how their data is collected.

Browser Sandboxing

Sandboxing restricts what website content can access on a user's device. It helps prevent potentially harmful code from freely accessing system resources.

Some browsers also isolate websites in separate processes. This can help limit the impact of a compromised page and improve overall browser security.

Cookies and Tracking

Cookies are small pieces of data that websites store in a browser. They can support useful features such as keeping users signed in, remembering preferences, and maintaining shopping carts.

However, cookies can also be used to track browsing activity. Third-party tracking can allow information about a user's activity to be collected across different websites.

Browser Fingerprinting

Browser fingerprinting uses characteristics of a browser or device, such as its configuration and supported features, to help identify or recognize users. It can sometimes be used even when cookies are restricted.

Private Browsing

Private browsing generally limits the browsing history and site data saved locally after a private session ends. It does not make users completely anonymous.

Websites, network administrators, and internet service providers may still be able to observe certain activity. Private browsing should therefore not be treated as a replacement for other security and privacy protections.

Protecting Yourself Online

  • Keep your browser updated.
  • Check website addresses before entering sensitive information.
  • Review browser privacy and site-permission settings.
  • Be cautious when opening unfamiliar links or downloads.
  • Understand the limitations of private browsing.
